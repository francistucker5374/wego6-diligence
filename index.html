The trust problem

What a buyer finds when they check the brand

This is the OZEM+ and Coolizi pattern again, and it is public:

  • Trustpilot: 2.7 out of 5 from 231 reviews. (Careful — those "2 of 5" page titles in the SERP are page numbers, not the rating. The 2.7 comes from Trustpilot's own structured data.)
  • verbraucherschutz.de publishes "Wego6 is not endorsed by Höhle der Löwen".
  • Verbraucherzentrale names it in its fake-celebrity-advertising warning — and that page ranks #3 for "wego6 seriös" and #4 for "wego6 kaufen". A consumer body outranks every affiliate on the money query.
  • stern.de ran an article on the fake Höhle der Löwen appearance; aponet.de carries Eckart von Hirschhausen's warning about his likeness being used.
  • Buyer complaints centre on a subscription trap, repeated charges, and refused refunds despite the advertised guarantee.

The funnel we would send traffic to carries fabricated testimonials ("Emily B., Berlin — verified review"), a "4.8 stars · 50,000 customers" claim, and no company imprint at all — the NL checkout names no legal entity, no address, no VAT number. The retail version sold through the pharmacies does name one: Glory Products OÜ, Järvevana tee 9, Tallinn, Estonia.

What this means for the build

We cannot write a glowing review of this product and stay on the right side of it. What we can do is what has actually worked for us before: own the "seriös / Betrug / is it legit" question honestly, rate it moderately, tell people the truth about the fake TV endorsement — and still carry the affiliate link for readers who decide to buy anyway. Competitors are already occupying that angle, so it's a race, not a blue ocean.
